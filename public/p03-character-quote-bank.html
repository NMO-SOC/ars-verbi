Every quotation below has been checked word for word against the text of the collection. Quotations are grouped by story and by character, with a note on where each one comes from. Where a character speaks, the words are theirs; where the line is narration or a character’s thoughts, the note says so.

‘The Daemon Lover’

The protagonist (unnamed, thirty-four)

  • ‘Anne, by the time you get this I will be married.’ – the letter to her sister, which she tears up.

  • She ‘was not satisfied with her clothes, her face, her apartment.’ – narration, at two minutes past ten.

  • She ‘tried to think of Jamie and could not see his face clearly, or hear his voice.’ – narration, while waiting.

  • ‘Please don’t make any mistake... I’m afraid you don’t understand.’ – to the superintendent’s wife, in a voice ‘with thirty-four years of accumulated pride’.

  • ‘Why don’t I go to the police... What a fool I’d look like.’ – her thoughts, deciding against it.

Jamie Harris (seen only through others)

  • ‘He’s rather tall, and fair. He wears a blue suit very often. He’s a writer.’ – her description of him.

  • ‘This is a big day for me, kid’ – the words the boy says Jamie spoke to him.

The strangers

  • ‘You got the wrong house, lady’ and, more quietly, ‘or the wrong guy’ – the superintendent, before he and his wife laugh.

  • ‘Now how many men in blue suits go past here every day, lady?’ – the delicatessen man.

  • ‘I hope you find your young man’ – the florist, who ‘gave it a nasty sound’.

  • ‘You gonna divorce him, missus? You got something on him?’ – the boy, running off with her dollar.

‘Like Mother Used to Make’

David Turner

  • ‘David Turner, who did everything in small quick movements’ – the opening line.

  • ‘it pleased him to have only one key to his home, and that safely in his own pocket’ – narration.

  • ‘I like things this way.’ – to Marcia, over dinner.

  • ‘You ought to keep your home neater.’ – to Marcia.

  • ‘Guess I better be getting along, Marcia.’ – instead of telling Mr Harris the truth.

  • ‘Thanks for a simply wonderful dinner, Marcia’ – leaving his own apartment.

Marcia

  • ‘I didn’t forget, Davie, I’m just late as usual. What’s for dinner?’ – arriving.

  • ‘Someone should teach me, I guess.’ – on keeping house.

  • ‘I haven’t made but two, three pies before’ – claiming David’s pie in front of Mr Harris.

  • ‘I don’t think Davie liked it much... I think it was too sour for him.’ – said ‘wickedly’.

  • ‘Don’t forget your key.’ – handing David the key to her apartment.

Mr Harris

  • ‘I just thought I’d stop by.’ – arriving uninvited.

  • ‘I like a sour pie... A cherry pie’s got to be sour.’ – as he ‘looked suspiciously at David’.

‘The Villager’

Miss Hilda Clarence

  • Her novel she ‘had read enthusiastically up to page fifty and only carried now for effect’ – narration.

  • In letters home she ‘referred to herself as a “Village die-hard.”’ – narration.

  • ‘Someday I’ll live there, she thought’ – looking at a building ‘crowned with flower gardens’.

  • ‘Not as easy as it used to be, she thought, it catches you in the shoulders.’ – trying the dancer’s pose.

  • ‘Dancer,’ she said. – answering Harris’s question.

  • ‘Artie’s going to Paris’ – taking on Mrs Roberts’s life.

  • ‘Her shoulders ached.’ – the final line.

Harris

  • ‘Or, rather, I hope to write’ – introducing himself.

  • ‘This place is certainly nicer than mine... Mine’s a hole in the wall.’

  • ‘What I need is a wife.’

‘The Witch’

Johnny, the little boy

  • ‘I saw a witch... There was a big old ugly old bad old witch outside.’ – to his mother.

  • ‘I’m a man already’ – to the stranger.

  • ‘Mr. Jesus’ – when the stranger asks his name.

  • ‘Did you cut her all in pieces?’ – asked ‘breathlessly’.

  • ‘Prob’ly he was a witch.’ – the final line.

The stranger

  • ‘Hello yourself, son.’ – his first words.

  • ‘All men smoke cigars... Someday you’ll smoke a cigar, too.’

  • ‘I bought her a rocking-horse and a doll and a million lollipops’ – the start of his story about his sister.

  • ‘Did I frighten you?’ – to the mother, before he and the boy laugh.

The mother

  • ‘Not much longer now’ – her answer ‘each time’ the boy asks how far they have to go.

  • ‘Just what do you think you’re doing?... Get out of here.’ – to the stranger.

  • ‘He was just teasing... Just teasing.’ – to Johnny.

  • ‘You sit still and be a good boy. You may have another lollipop.’

‘The Renegade’

Mrs Walpole

  • ‘That’s perfectly ridiculous’ – on the phone, told her dog kills chickens.

  • ‘I just can’t believe it of Lady. She’s so gentle.’

  • ‘anyone would tell on a chicken-killing dog; but he doesn’t have to be so pleased about it’ – her thoughts about Mr White.

  • ‘But how did he feel about you?... Did he ever like you again?’ – to the grocer, about his father’s dog.

  • ‘feeling the harsh hands pulling her down, the sharp points closing in on her throat’ – the final line.

The townspeople

  • ‘He’s been killing my chickens.’ – the caller; ‘The voice sounded satisfied now; Mrs. Walpole had been cornered.’

  • ‘You’ll have to do something about the dog.’ – the caller.

  • ‘Guess you’re not going to have any more dog.’ – Mr White, grinning.

  • ‘You get a dead chicken and tie it around the dog’s neck’ – Mr White.

  • ‘Sure... Shoot him.’ – the man at the grocery, asked if the dog can be cured.

  • ‘Scratch his eyes out... He wouldn’t ever be able to see another chicken.’ – the grocer.

Jack and Judy (the twins)

  • ‘She’s going to get shot.’ – Judy.

  • ‘Then we put this real sharp spiky collar around your neck’ – Jack, before he ‘kissed the top of her head’.

  • ‘The spikes cut her head off’ – Jack.

  • ‘They both began to laugh’ – narration, as Lady pants ‘as though she were laughing too’.

‘Charles’

Laurie

  • ‘Isn’t anybody here?’ – coming home from his first day.

  • ‘I didn’t learn nothing’ – to his father.

  • ‘Today Charles hit the teacher.’ – said as he ‘grinned enormously’.

  • ‘Look at my thumb. Gee, you’re dumb.’ – to his father.

  • ‘Hi, Pop, y’old dust mop.’

Laurie’s mother (the narrator)

  • ‘a long-trousered, swaggering character who forgot to stop at the corner and wave good-bye to me’ – on Laurie’s first morning.

  • ‘this Charles boy sounds like such a bad influence’ – to her husband.

  • ‘I wanted passionately to meet Charles’s mother’ – narration.

  • ‘I suppose this time it’s Charles’s influence.’ – to the teacher.

Laurie’s father and the teacher

  • ‘Bound to be people like Charles in the world. Might as well meet them now as later.’ – Laurie’s father.

  • ‘We had a little trouble adjusting, the first week or so... but now he’s a fine little helper. With occasional lapses, of course.’ – the teacher, about Laurie.

  • ‘We don’t have any Charles in the kindergarten.’ – the teacher.

‘After You, My Dear Alphonse’

Mrs Wilson

  • ‘Boyd will eat anything.’

  • ‘Boyd wants to grow up and be a big strong man so he can work hard’

  • ‘Does he...work?’ – about Boyd’s father, after she ‘hesitated’.

  • She ‘restrained an impulse to pat Boyd on the head.’ – narration.

  • ‘There are many little boys like you, Boyd, who would be very grateful for the clothes someone was kind enough to give them.’ – taking the gingerbread away.

  • ‘Don’t think I’m angry, Boyd. I’m just disappointed in you, that’s all.’

Boyd

  • ‘How do you do, Mrs. Wilson?’

  • ‘My father eats anything he wants to’

  • ‘But I have plenty of clothes, thank you’ – refusing the bundle.

  • ‘Is your mother still mad?’ – asked ‘in a low voice’.

Johnny

  • ‘Dead Japanese... We stand them in the ground and run over them with tanks.’ – explaining the wood.

  • ‘Boyd’s father doesn’t have to... He’s a foreman.’

  • ‘Why should she?... You don’t work.’ – to his mother, about Boyd’s mother.

  • ‘She’s screwy sometimes.’ (Boyd: ‘So’s mine.’)

‘Flower Garden’

Young Mrs Winning (Helen)

  • ‘It seems as though spring would never come... I get so tired of the cold.’ – the opening.

  • ‘This could have been mine, she was thinking’ – in the cottage.

  • ‘This dreary old place’ – of the Winning house.

  • ‘Children will call names... There’s not much you can do.’ – after Howard’s racist name-calling.

  • ‘You won’t have him working here past today, will you?’ – about Mr Jones.

  • ‘pretty soon they’ll stop coming to me first, they’ll tell someone else to speak to me’ – her thoughts.

  • ‘I sound exactly like Mother Winning, and realized, I’m enjoying this’ – her thoughts, as Mrs MacLane asks for help.

  • ‘The nerve of her, trying to blame the colored folks.’ – her thoughts.

  • She ‘swung around without speaking and started, with great dignity, back up the hill toward the old Winning house.’ – the final line.

Mrs MacLane

  • ‘I love gardens.’

  • ‘I don’t think I’ve ever been so happy.’

  • ‘Go tell that boy you’re sorry’ – to Davey.

  • ‘What I wanted to ask you is, what on earth is gone wrong?’

  • ‘Are you sure it isn’t because of Mr. Jones working here?’

  • ‘Do you think I ought to give it up, Mr. Jones? Go back to the city where I’ll never have to see another garden?’

  • ‘Leave it for the next people to move!’

The town

  • ‘Not that kind of help’ – the elder Mrs Winning.

  • ‘you ought to talk to her about that colored man working there’ – the elder Mrs Winning.

  • ‘He’s there every blessed day... And working out there without his shirt on.’ – the elder Mrs Winning.

  • ‘Where’s that dressed-up friend of yours get to?’ – Mrs Harris.

  • ‘I just thought you might mind if he didn’t come.’ – Mrs Burton, about Davey and the birthday party.

‘Elizabeth’

Elizabeth Style

  • ‘Just before the alarm went off she was lying in a hot sunny garden’ – the opening.

  • ‘the ugly morning noises of people stirring’ – narration.

  • ‘I wish I’d been a blonde’ – her thought at the mirror.

  • ‘Is he trying in his own heavy-handed fashion to beautify the office?’ – her thoughts about Daphne’s hiring.

  • ‘good old Harris, he never has a chance if you talk fast’ – her thoughts after the phone call.

  • ‘I’m afraid Mr. Shax didn’t have courage enough to tell you’ – dismissing Daphne.

  • ‘something wonderful would happen to change her whole life’ – narration, before Harris arrives.

  • ‘while she was planning her new life she forgot Jim Harris, his heavy face, his thin voice’ – narration.

Others

  • ‘Dried-up old maid’ – a woman on the bus.

  • ‘there was no one around to tell Elizabeth Style’ – narration, on her arrival in New York at twenty.

  • ‘Mr. Shax didn’t tell me anything’ – Daphne Hill.

  • ‘A suit like yours?’ – Daphne, looking Elizabeth up and down.

‘The Tooth’

Clara Spencer

  • ‘I feel so funny’ – to her husband at the bus.

  • ‘Milkman... Leave a note telling him we want eggs.’ – her last words to him.

  • Her tooth ‘seemed now the only part of her to have any identity.’ – narration.

  • ‘you’d think my own face would know me’ – her thoughts at the mirror.

  • ‘Clara... Clara?’ – reading her own name on her barrette.

  • ‘her hand in Jim’s and her hair down on her shoulders, she ran barefoot through hot sand.’ – the final line.

Her husband and Jim

  • ‘You had a toothache on our honeymoon’ – her husband, ‘accusingly’.

  • ‘It’s only a toothache.’ – her husband.

  • ‘My name is Jim’ – the stranger.

  • ‘Even farther than Samarkand... and the waves ringing on the shore like bells.’ – Jim.

  • ‘The sand is so white it looks like snow’ – Jim.

  • ‘The grass is so green and so soft’ – Jim, on the escalator.

‘Of Course’

Mrs Tylor

  • ‘Nice people, obviously.’ – her judgement from a tan suit.

  • ‘There’s something so nice about congenial people right next door.’

  • ‘Of course... No radio.’

  • ‘feeling as though she had been saying nothing else all morning’ – narration.

  • ‘I’ll go with you, dear.’ – the final line, agreeing to take Carol to the movies.

Mrs Harris

  • ‘James does not go to movies.’

  • ‘Mr. Harris... feels that movies are intellectually retarding.’

  • ‘Mr. Harris just feels that the newspapers are a mass degradation of taste.’

  • ‘It’s terribly hard for people to understand the desire for a really peaceful life.’

  • ‘You’ll be thinking my husband is crazy.’

  • ‘Pre-Elizabethan, of course.’ – the only plays her husband reads.

‘The Lottery’

Tessie Hutchinson

  • ‘Clean forgot what day it was’ – arriving late.

  • ‘Wouldn’t have me leave m’dishes in the sink, now, would you, Joe?’

  • ‘Get up there, Bill.’

  • ‘You didn’t give him time enough to take any paper he wanted. I saw you. It wasn’t fair!’

  • ‘There’s Don and Eva... Make them take their chance!’ – trying to add her married daughter’s household.

  • ‘It isn’t fair, it isn’t right’ – her last words.

Mr Summers and Mr Graves

  • People ‘were sorry for him, because he had no children and his wife was a scold.’ – narration, on Mr Summers.

  • ‘Little late today, folks.’ – Mr Summers.

  • ‘Take your time, son.’ – Mr Summers, to the Watson boy.

  • ‘Daughters draw with their husbands’ families, Tessie’ – Mr Summers.

  • ‘Let’s finish quickly.’ – Mr Summers, once Tessie’s paper is shown.

  • ‘Harry, you help little Dave.’ – Mr Summers; Mr Graves holds the child’s slip.

Old Man Warner

  • ‘Pack of crazy fools’ – on villages giving up the lottery.

  • ‘Lottery in June, corn be heavy soon.’

  • ‘There’s always been a lottery’

  • ‘Seventy-seventh year I been in the lottery’

  • ‘Come on, come on, everyone.’

The other villagers

  • ‘Shut up, Tessie.’ – Bill Hutchinson, who later ‘forced the slip of paper out of her hand’.

  • ‘Be a good sport, Tessie’ – Mrs Delacroix.

  • ‘All of us took the same chance.’ – Mrs Graves.

  • Mrs Delacroix ‘selected a stone so large she had to pick it up with both hands’ – narration.

  • ‘I can’t run at all. You’ll have to go ahead and I’ll catch up with you.’ – Mrs Dunbar.

  • ‘Some places have already quit lotteries’ – Mrs Adams; at the end, ‘Steve Adams was in the front of the crowd’.
