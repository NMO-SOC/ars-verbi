Use this grid when you plan a Section A essay. Find the idea in the topic, read down the column, and choose the sequences where it is central for your main evidence. The sequence numbers match the Scene Summaries and Analyses page, and the evidence under the grid shows where to look. Key: •• = central to the sequence; • = present and usable; blank = not a strong choice for that idea.

Column headings: Nature = nature as a fundamental force; Reason = the human need for rationality and control; Time = time, measured and timeless; Class = class and civilisation; Appearance = beauty, innocence and appearance; Restraint = femininity, restraint and release. The first five match the Thematic Quote Bank; the sixth draws on the Values page.

Nature as a Fundamental Force

Central: 1, 3, 4, 7, 11.

  • 1: the Rock emerges from the mist before we see the college, and Miranda’s voice-over says ‘What we see and what we seem are but a dream, a dream within a dream.’

  • 3: Irma, as the Rock comes into view: ‘Waiting a million years, just for us.’

  • 4: the narrowing passages draw the girls upward; Edith: ‘Why can’t we just sit on this log and look at the ugly old rock from here?’

  • 7: the Rock overwhelms Michael, who is found dazed and injured, clutching a scrap of lace.

  • 11: Mrs Appleyard, the champion of order, is found at the base of the Rock; the voice-over says ‘she fell while attempting to climb the rock’.

  • Present: the cultivated flowers of Sequence 2; Edith’s memory of a red cloud in 6; Michael’s ‘It comes back at night in dreams’; Albert’s dream of Sara in 10.

The Human Need for Rationality and Control

Central: 2, 5, 6, 8.

  • 2: Mrs Appleyard’s warnings about gloves, snakes and ‘tomboy foolishness’.

  • 5: Mr Hussey: ‘No one knows. No one saw her leave. We were asleep.’

  • 6: Sergeant Bumpher asks Edith, ‘Was there a man?’; Mrs Bumpher insists, ‘People just don’t disappear, my dear. Not without good reason.’

  • 8: the doctor finds Irma’s condition ‘very strange’, and the townspeople shout, ‘We’ve got a bloody right to know.’

  • Present: Miss McCraw’s ‘Must be something magnetic’ (3); Marion’s measurements (4); Michael’s intuition against Albert’s advice (7); the girls’ demand for answers (9); Mrs Appleyard’s grip on the college (10); her question about Miss McCraw being ‘spirited away’ (11).

Time

Central: 3, 4, 11.

  • 3: Mr Hussey: ‘Blowed if me watch hasn’t stopped.’ Miss McCraw: ‘Never stopped before. Must be something magnetic.’ Miranda rejects her watch: ‘Can’t stand the ticking above my heart.’

  • 4: Miranda: ‘Everything begins and ends at exactly the right time and place’; Edith: ‘When are we going home?’

  • 11: Mrs Appleyard remembers Bournemouth, where ‘Nothing changed. Ever.’; the slow-motion closing images hold the picnic in time.

  • Present: the title card’s exact date (1); Mrs Appleyard’s expectation that the party will be back ‘for a light supper’ (2); the drag’s late return (5).

Class and Civilisation

Central: 2, 3, 9, 10.

  • 2: gloves, corsets and rules; Sara is told she cannot go on the picnic.

  • 3: cake, china and white dresses laid out beneath the ancient Rock; Miss McCraw: ‘How foolish can human creatures be.’

  • 9: the ‘refined’ young ladies become a hysterical mob around Irma.

  • 10: parents withdraw their daughters, and Mrs Appleyard tells Sara she will be returned to the orphanage.

  • Present: the college framed by wild grass (1); Marion’s ‘Like a lot of ants’ from the Rock (4); the town’s ‘It couldn’t be local’ (6); Michael and Albert’s friendship across class (7); Mrs Appleyard’s concern for the college’s name (8); Bournemouth (11).

Beauty, Innocence and Appearance

Central: 2, 8, 9.

  • 2: the girls in white, exchanging Valentines; flowers everywhere.

  • 8: Irma, shrouded in white gauze; when Mademoiselle visits, she finds her ‘so pale but prettier than ever’.

  • 9: Irma returns in a vivid red dress, set against the girls in white.

  • Present: ‘what we see and what we seem’ (1); white dresses at the picnic (3); Mademoiselle’s ‘Botticelli angel’; Mrs Appleyard’s lie about Sara’s guardian (10); the final wave from Miranda (11).

Femininity, Restraint and Release

Central: 2, 4, 6, 9.

  • 2: the girls lace one another’s corsets.

  • 4: Miranda, Marion and Irma go on without shoes and stockings.

  • 6: the investigation fixates on a man and on Miss McCraw’s missing skirt.

  • 9: Sara strapped to the gymnasium wall, ‘for her own good’.

  • Present: gloves removed after Woodend (3); the scrap of lace (7); the doctor’s examination of Irma (8); Miss Lumley’s departure (10); Mrs Appleyard dressed more softly than ever before (11).

Strong Combinations

  • Nature against civilisation: 1, 3, 4, 11.

  • The failure of reason: 5, 6, 8, 9.

  • Time, measured and timeless: 3, 4, 11.

  • Institutional cruelty: 2, 9, 10.
