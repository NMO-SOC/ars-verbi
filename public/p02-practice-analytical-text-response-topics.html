Practice Analytical Text Response Topics

Section A topics on Picnic at Hanging Rock tend to take one of three shapes. Learn to recognise them in reading time, because each asks you to do something slightly different.

Task type What it asks Typical wording
Construction How Weir builds an idea through the film: framing, sound, costume, setting, structure. How does Weir explore / depict / portray / highlight / challenge...?
Relationship How two ideas connect: cause and effect, contrast, comparison or connection. ...causes, shapes, leads to, limits; more than, as much as; despite, both...and
Idea / message What the film suggests, reveals or demonstrates about an idea. The film suggests that...; There are no...; ...is rarely...; ...is not necessarily...

The four big ideas that sit underneath almost every topic are nature as a fundamental force, the human need for rationality and control, time, and class and civilisation. Topics will not always name these ideas directly, so practise translating unexpected wording back into them. 1. How does Weir portray nature as mysterious in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 2. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, appearances are carefully maintained, but rarely reflect reality. Discuss. 3. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, answers are unattainable. Discuss. 4. How does Weir portray the Australian landscape as mysterious in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 5. How does Weir explore the tension between human civilisation and the natural world in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 6. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, the natural world is ultimately unknowable. Discuss. 7. How does Weir explore the human desire to understand the world in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 8. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, the need to explain events is driven more by a desire for control than a desire for truth. Do you agree? 9. Picnic at Hanging Rock suggests that reason cannot solve every problem. Discuss. 10. How does Weir portray the limitations of authority in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 11. The greatest threat to social order in Picnic at Hanging Rock comes not from nature but from within society itself. Discuss. 12. Picnic at Hanging Rock suggests that social rules exist only to create the illusion of order. Discuss. 13. How does Weir explore different perceptions of time in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 14. How does Weir contrast the timelessness of nature and the fleeting nature of human existence in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 15. Picnic at Hanging Rock suggests that human existence is insignificant in the vastness of time. Discuss. 16. How does Weir depict the natural world as a source of both fascination and fear? 17. Picnic at Hanging Rock suggests that nature reveals the limitations of human civilisation. Discuss. 18. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, social conventions restrict individual freedom. Discuss. 19. How does Weir explore the limits to human understanding in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 20. Weir suggests that civilisation is far more fragile than it appears. Discuss. 21. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, the passing of time deepens rather than resolves the mystery of the girls’ disappearance. Discuss. 22. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, the desire for certainty creates more problems than it solves. Discuss. 23. How does Weir explore the relationship between freedom and vulnerability in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 24. ‘The greatest threat to civilisation comes from within.’ To what extent is this true in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 25. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, people are imprisoned by their need to understand the world. Discuss. 26. How does Picnic at Hanging Rock explore the tension between the security of the familiar and the attraction of the unknown? 27. How does Picnic at Hanging Rock explore the importance of social status and appearances? 28. Picnic at Hanging Rock suggests that society can be disrupted but nature is timeless. Do you agree? 29. How does Weir explore the relationship between knowledge and control in Picnic at Hanging Rock? 30. How do events in Picnic at Hanging Rock expose true natures? 31. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, crossing boundaries can be both liberating and dangerous. Discuss. 32. ‘Nothing changed – ever.’ Picnic at Hanging Rock suggests that nature is both timeless and disruptive. To what extent do you agree? 33. In Picnic at Hanging Rock, the desire for control leads to disorder. Discuss. 34. ‘What we see and what we seem are but a dream.’ In Picnic at Hanging Rock, nothing is quite what it seems. Discuss. 35. ‘There’s some questions got answers, some haven’t.’ Picnic at Hanging Rock suggests that we must learn to live with uncertainty. Do you agree? 36. ‘People don’t just disappear... not without good reason.’ The search for reasons in Picnic at Hanging Rock reveals more about the searchers than about the lost. Discuss. 37. ‘How foolish can human creatures be.’ Picnic at Hanging Rock is a critique of human arrogance. Discuss. 38. ‘We shall only be gone a little while.’ The characters of Picnic at Hanging Rock underestimate the forces beyond their control. Do you agree?
