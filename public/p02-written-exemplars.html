Sample 1: Model Picnic at Hanging Rock Essay

Topic: How does Weir depict the natural world as a source of both fascination and fear? Introduction

In Picnic at Hanging Rock (1975), Peter Weir constructs the Australian bush as a place that draws people in at the very moment it threatens to consume them. Through his framing of the ancient Rock, his treatment of time and the haunting soundscape of the film, Weir presents a landscape that is at once enchanting and menacing to the members of colonial society who visit it. Ultimately, the film suggests that nature’s allure and its danger come from the same source: it lies beyond the reach of human understanding and control. Body Paragraph 1

From the opening sequence, Weir establishes the Rock as an alluring yet ominous presence that overshadows the civilised world. The film begins not with Appleyard College but with the Rock emerging slowly from mist, accompanied by Miranda’s voice-over: ‘what we see and what we seem are but a dream, a dream within a dream.’ By placing the Rock first, before the factual title card and the image of the college, Weir signals that nature is the primary force in the film, older and more powerful than the society that tries to explain it. As the girls climb, low-angle shots make the pinnacles tower over them, while narrow passages enclose them in a labyrinth of stone. The girls are enthralled, gazing upwards as if hypnotised, yet the same framing diminishes them, rendering them vulnerable within a landscape that seems to be swallowing them. Through this framing, Weir positions viewers to share the girls’ fascination while sensing the threat they cannot see. This is how the film makes nature’s allure and its danger inseparable. Body Paragraph 2

Weir further portrays nature as captivating and frightening by showing that it operates outside the measured, human time on which civilisation depends. Mrs Appleyard’s instruction to return ‘at about 8’ and Irma’s confidence that they will be back ‘long before tea’ reflect a society organised by schedules. At the Rock, however, these certainties dissolve: Mr Hussey’s and Miss McCraw’s watches stop at twelve, and Miss McCraw can only speculate that there ‘must be something magnetic.’ For Miranda, this escape from clock time is liberating. She cannot ‘stand the ticking above [her] heart’ and, at the summit, declares that ‘everything begins and ends at exactly the right time and place.’ For Edith, by contrast, the loss of ordinary time is terrifying, as her repeated plea, ‘when are we going home?’, reveals. Weir thus shows that the timelessness of nature is fascinating to those willing to surrender to it and frightening to those who cling to human order. Body Paragraph 3

The soundscape of the film reinforces this duality, making the bush seem both enchanting and unsettling. Gheorghe Zamfir’s pan flute drifts over images of the girls crossing the stream, imbuing the landscape with an ethereal, romantic beauty and associating it with freedom from the restrictions of the college. Yet beneath this music Weir layers a low, insistent rumble that seems to emanate from the Rock itself, and as Edith flees, her screams merge with a shrieking, discordant noise. These sounds destabilise the idyllic images and evoke a sense of forces beyond human comprehension. Even after Irma is found, an eerie rush of wind through the rocks suggests that nature’s mystery remains intact. Through this contrast between seductive melody and menacing sound, Weir ensures that the audience never fully trusts the beauty of the bush. Conclusion

Weir’s Picnic at Hanging Rock depicts the natural world as a source of fascination precisely because it is a source of fear. Through framing that dwarfs the characters, a treatment of time that defies human measurement and a soundscape that blends beauty with menace, Weir presents a landscape that invites surrender while threatening destruction. In refusing to explain the disappearance, the film leaves its audience in the same position as its characters: drawn to a mystery that cannot be solved.

Sample 2: Paragraph Rewrite

Topic: In Picnic at Hanging Rock, the passing of time deepens rather than resolves the mystery of the girls’ disappearance. Discuss. Original paragraph (does not fully respond to the topic)

Irma’s return provides hope that the mystery of the other girls’ disappearance might eventually be solved. When Irma is found, the townspeople gather together to find answers and renew the search. One man even shouts, ‘we’ve got a bloody right to know,’ showing that people are still determined to discover what happened. However, Irma cannot remember what happened to her on the Rock. The doctor also describes Irma’s condition as ‘very strange,’ while Mrs Appleyard says that her return ‘makes it worse.’ These examples show that Irma’s return does not solve the mystery and that the characters still have lots of unanswered questions. What is wrong with it?

  • The topic sentence argues the opposite of the topic (hope of resolution) and never mentions time.

  • It shows that questions remain unanswered, but not how Irma’s return creates new questions.

  • It does not explain why Mrs Appleyard says Irma’s return ‘makes it worse’.

  • It ends by summarising events rather than making a larger point about Weir’s message.

Rewritten paragraph

Rather than bringing the investigation closer to the truth, Irma’s return a week after the picnic only multiplies the questions surrounding the disappearance. As news spreads, the townspeople gather outside Bumpher’s house, and one man’s insistence that ‘we’ve got a bloody right to know’ reveals a community that assumes time and effort will inevitably produce answers. Yet Irma cannot remember what happened on the Rock, and the doctor’s verdict that her condition is ‘very strange’ turns the one surviving witness into a fresh mystery. Mrs Appleyard’s response, that the discovery ‘makes it worse’, is telling: a single survivor with no story removes the possibility of a simple ending, leaving the fate of the others more uncertain than before. Weir underscores this as Irma recuperates shrouded beneath white gauze, an image that veils her as completely as the mist veils the Rock. Through Irma’s return, Weir suggests that the passage of time does not erode a mystery but entrenches it, exposing the limits of a society that believes everything can be explained.

Sample 3: Essay Plan

Topic: How does Weir explore the relationship between knowledge and control in Picnic at Hanging Rock? This topic has two ideas, and both must be addressed. You might show how characters are motivated more by one than the other, how the desire for one leads to the other, or how the two come into conflict. Contention

Weir suggests that characters seek knowledge in order to gain control, but that the Rock exposes the limits of both, forcing them to confront a world that cannot be understood or mastered. BP1: People initially seek knowledge as a way of gaining control over the world.

  • Miss McCraw studies the Rock through geometry and geology – she tries to understand nature through measurement and logic.

  • Marion wants to ‘make a few measurements of the rock’ – knowledge as a way of claiming the landscape.

  • Mrs Appleyard’s warnings about snakes, ants and ‘tomboy foolishness’ – knowing the dangers in advance is meant to keep the girls safe and controlled.

  • Sergeant Bumpher questions witnesses (‘Was there a man?’) – seeks a rational explanation that will restore order.

BP2: Weir shows that attempts to gain knowledge often reveal the limits of human control.

  • Michael changes his memory from three girls to four – memory and perception are unreliable.

  • Bumpher dismisses Edith’s red cloud and fixates on Miss McCraw’s missing skirt – the authorities accept only knowledge that fits their worldview.

  • The watches stop at twelve: ‘Must be something magnetic’ – the instruments of control fail.

  • The doctor finds Irma’s condition ‘very strange’ – investigation deepens rather than solves the mystery.

BP3: Ultimately, people must accept that complete knowledge and control are impossible – and those who cannot accept this are destroyed.

  • Mrs Appleyard says finding only one girl ‘makes it worse’ – uncertainty threatens her need for order.

  • Her control turns to cruelty towards Sara, and her college collapses.

  • The head gardener: ‘There’s some questions got answers and some haven’t’ – acceptance of uncertainty.

  • Mrs Appleyard dies ‘while attempting to climb the rock’ – the champion of control is consumed by what she could not understand.

Section A Sample Essays: Picnic at Hanging Rock (Low, Medium and High)

Topic: Weir suggests that the order imposed by Appleyard College cannot contain the natural world. To what extent do you agree?
Sample Essay I (Low)

Introduction

In Picnic at Hanging Rock, Peter Weir shows a school trying to control nature and failing. I agree with the statement because the college’s rules, its clocks and its sounds all break down when the girls go to the Rock. Weir uses setting, time and sound to show this.
Body Paragraph 1

Weir shows that the college’s rules mean little against the power of nature. Mrs Appleyard has many rules for the picnic. She tells the girls to return ‘at about 8’ and warns them about snakes. This shows the college wants to control the girls.Ideas & interpretationLabelled as analysis but it only restates the rule. Say how the instruction controls the girls: what they are expected to do, and what would happen if they did not. However, when they get to the Rock the rules do not work anymoreStructure & evidenceThe instinct to turn to a counter-point is right, but 'however' is a signpost with no argument behind it. Explain why the rules fail, not just that they do. because the Rock is very big. Weir shows the Rock in big shots so it looks powerful.
Body Paragraph 2 The rules meant to protect the girls are the first thing the Rock overrides.

Time is another way the college tries to control things. Mr Hussey’s and Miss McCraw’s watches stop at twelve, and Miss McCraw says it ‘must be something magnetic’. This shows the Rock is stronger than the clocks. Irma says they will be back ‘long before tea’, but they never come back, so the college’s plans fail.
Body Paragraph 3 So the order the college relies on fails at the very point it is meant to hold.

Weir’s soundtrack makes nature seem both beautiful and dangerous. The music is calm at first, then there is a scary noise.Language & techniqueDescribes what happens without naming the technique or what it does to the audience. Name the soundtrack element and the effect. This shows nature is beautiful but also dangerous. When Edith runs, she screams, which makes the audience feel scared.
Conclusion The threat of the Rock is therefore something the audience hears as well as sees.

Weir shows the college trying to control nature and failing. The rules, the time and the sound all show this. Therefore I agree with the statement.Structure & evidenceThe conclusion repeats the contention with no new idea. The question asks 'to what extent', and no degree of agreement is given.
Sample Essay II (Medium)

Introduction

In Picnic at Hanging Rock, Peter Weir presents Appleyard College as a place built on order: its timetables, its rules about gloves and punctuality, its certainty about when the girls will return. I agree that the film shows this order failing against nature, though the failure is not a simple victory for the Rock. Weir uses time, framing and sound to show an ordered world losing its hold on the people inside it.
Body Paragraph 1

The college lives by the clock, and the picnic is planned around itIdeas & interpretationA clear topic sentence that makes a claim about the topic, not just a description of the setting.. Mrs Appleyard tells the girls to return ‘at about 8’, and Irma is confident they will be back ‘long before tea’. At the Rock, though, the clocks stop. Mr Hussey’s and Miss McCraw’s watches stop at twelve, and Miss McCraw can only suggest that it ‘must be something magnetic’. The mechanical measures of the college become meaninglessLanguage & techniquePrecise word choice that turns the stopped watches into a claim about the college's whole way of life. in a place that seems to run on a different time.
Body Paragraph 2 The college’s order therefore depends on a time that the Rock does not share.

Weir also reduces the girls’ authority through framing. As they climb, low-angle shots make the pinnacles tower over them, and narrow passages enclose them. The girls are shown gazing upwards, enthralled, yet the same shots make them look small. The college’s rules about gloves and snakes are trivial beside a landscape that seems to be swallowing them.
Body Paragraph 3 Framing, then, turns the college’s authority into something the landscape can overwhelm.

The soundtrack completes the picture. Gheorghe Zamfir’s pan flute makes the bush seem romantic, but beneath it Weir layers a low rumble that seems to come from the Rock. When Edith flees, her screams merge with a shrieking noise. Beauty and threat are never separated, so the audience cannot fully trust the bush.
Conclusion The soundtrack therefore carries the film’s argument that order cannot contain nature.

Weir presents Appleyard College as an order that cannot survive contact with the Rock. Its clocks stop, its rules shrink and its voices are drowned out by the sound of the bush. The film does not give nature a simple victory, however. The girls are not conquered so much as takenIdeas & interpretationA qualified conclusion: it accepts the statement while pointing out what the film does instead. This is what 'to what extent' rewards., and that is what makes the order’s failure so unsettling.
Sample Essay III (High)

Introduction

The opening of Picnic at Hanging Rock sets up its argument before the college appears at all. Weir begins with the Rock emerging from mist and Miranda’s voice-over: ‘What we see and what we seem are but a dream, a dream within a dream.’ I agree with the statement, but the film’s argument is harder than a story of defeat. The order of Appleyard College is not overpowered by the Rock. It is revealed as a fiction the moment it meets the bushIdeas & interpretationThe contention goes beyond 'the college fails'. It argues the order was never real, which makes the answer more than a yes.. Weir builds this through the opening, the stopped clocks and the soundtrack.
Body Paragraph 1

The opening makes that fiction visible. Before the title card gives its factual details, the Rock is shown first, and Miranda’s voice-over frames everything that follows as a dream. Weir inverts the usual hierarchyLanguage & techniqueA precise reading of the opening: the film reverses the expected order of authority between society and nature.: nature is presented as the older, more real authority, and the college is an afterthought. When the girls later climb the Rock, they walk into the same dream and accept it without question.
Body Paragraph 2 The opening therefore settles the film’s answer before the disappearance begins.

Time shows most clearly how the college’s order gives way to the Rock. The clocks provide the clearest evidence. Appleyard College runs on instructions to return ‘at about 8’, and Irma is sure they will be back ‘long before tea’. At the Rock, Mr Hussey’s and Miss McCraw’s watches stop at twelve, and Miss McCraw can only suggest that it ‘must be something magnetic’. Miranda goes further, rejecting her own watch because its ticking is unbearable, and at the summit she declares that ‘everything begins and ends at exactly the right time and place’. Weir shows the college’s measured time as a convention the Rock simply does not recognise.
Body Paragraph 3 The college’s clocks are the first thing the Rock makes meaningless.

Sound finishes what time begins. Gheorghe Zamfir’s pan flute drifts over the crossing of the stream, making the bush seem freely romantic, but Weir layers a low rumble beneath it and lets Edith’s screams merge with a shrieking discord. The audience is never allowed to rest in the beauty. Even after Irma is found, the rush of wind through the rocks keeps the question open. The college wants an explanation, and the film offers only sensationLanguage & techniqueA closing point that ties the argument to the film's refusal to explain the disappearance..
Conclusion That refusal to explain is what makes the college’s failure complete.

Weir’s film argues that order is a story people tell themselves, and the Rock refuses to co-operate with it. The opening dream, the stopped watches and the shrieking soundtrack each undermine the college’s authority in a different register. Because the film never explains the disappearance, the failure of Appleyard College is not a single moment. It is the condition the whole film describes.
