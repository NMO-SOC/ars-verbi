Scene Summaries and Analyses

The sequences below follow the order of the film. Use them to locate evidence quickly; the Analysis notes suggest how each sequence connects to the big ideas.

Sequence 1 : The Opening

Summary : Hanging Rock emerges from mist while Miranda’s voice-over says ‘What we see and what we seem are but a dream, a dream within a dream.’ A title card gives precise factual details about the disappearance. Appleyard College is then shown framed against a foreground of wild summer grass. Analysis : The order is significant. We see the Rock first, then the facts, then the college. From the outset Weir establishes the three forces of the film: nature as a fundamental force, the human need for factual order, and civilisation. The facts tell us what happened but not why, so the film begins with the mystery already unsolved.

Sequence 2 : St Valentine’s Morning

Summary : The girls dress, lace each other’s corsets, and exchange Valentine cards and poems. Miranda and Sara share a tender moment. Flowers are everywhere. Mrs Appleyard addresses the girls about gloves, snakes, ‘tomboy foolishness’ and returning ‘at about 8’. Sara is told she is not to go on the picnic. Analysis : Weir portrays the students as elegant, refined and innocent, but also sheltered, uniform and restrained. The cultivated flowers represent a version of nature that has been domesticated and controlled. Mrs Appleyard’s warnings reveal an institutional fear of disorder, danger and lost time.

Sequence 3 : The Picnic

Summary : The drag, driven by Mr Hussey, carries the girls, Miss McCraw and Mademoiselle to the Rock. Miranda opens a gate to let the party through. The picnic is laid out with cake, china and white dresses. Nearby, Colonel and Mrs Fitzhubert picnic with their nephew Michael and their groom Albert. Mr Hussey’s and Miss McCraw’s watches are found stopped at twelve. Much of the party grows drowsy and falls asleep. Analysis : The picnic and the characters’ clothing appear incongruous against the ancient landscape; civilised behaviour seems intrusive or strangely detached from the wilderness. The gate is the first threshold between the familiar world and the unknown. The stopped watches signal that the Rock operates outside measured, human time.

Sequence 4 : The Climb

Summary : Miranda, Marion, Irma and Edith get permission to take measurements at the base of the Rock. Michael watches them cross the stream; Albert makes crude remarks. The girls climb higher, the rocks becoming increasingly maze-like. Near the top they fall asleep, then wake. Miranda, Marion and Irma, now without shoes and stockings, walk between two great rocks. Edith screams and runs back down. Miss McCraw is seen climbing alone. Analysis : Successive thresholds (the gate, the stream, the narrowing passages) create the sense that the Rock is drawing the girls towards it. Miranda’s line that ‘everything begins and ends at exactly the right time and place’ suggests acceptance of a different kind of time, while Edith’s ‘when are we going home?’ clings to ordinary chronology. Shedding clothes signals liberation from social convention.

Sequence 5 : The Return of the Drag

Summary : The drag arrives back at the college late at night. Mrs Appleyard, who has been waiting, hears from Mr Hussey that ‘nobody knows what happened... we were all asleep.’ Analysis : The schedule Mrs Appleyard imposed has collapsed. Mr Hussey’s admission that everyone was asleep suggests that the rational, conscious mind was absent at the very moment of the event, an early sign that reason will not provide an answer.

Sequence 6 : The Investigation

Summary : Sergeant Bumpher leads the inquiry. Edith is examined by the doctor and asked ‘Was there a man?’ Mrs Bumpher reports that the town believes ‘it couldn’t be local.’ Michael first says he saw three girls, then four. Asked what he was thinking of as the girls crossed the creek, he points to a swan vase. Edith returns to the Rock and remembers a red cloud and Miss McCraw climbing in her drawers; Bumpher fixates on the missing skirt. Search parties, a tracker and dogs comb the Rock. Analysis : Each line of inquiry looks for an explanation that fits society’s assumptions: a male attacker, an outsider, a sexual crime. Memory proves unreliable. Bumpher’s dismissal of the red cloud shows that the authorities seek only the kind of explanation that fits their worldview. The film suggests rational thinking can be more concerned with preserving a sense of control than with discovering the truth.

Sequence 7 : Michael’s Search

Summary : Against Albert’s advice, Michael rides to the Rock and searches alone, staying overnight. He becomes disoriented, struggles to climb and falls asleep. Albert finds him dazed and injured, clutching a scrap of lace. Following Michael’s trail, Albert discovers Irma alive. Analysis : Michael succeeds where the official searches failed, perhaps because he is guided by emotion and intuition rather than logic. Yet the Rock also overwhelms him, rewarding curiosity while frustrating the desire for certainty.

Sequence 8 : Irma Recovers

Summary : Irma recuperates at the Fitzhuberts’ home, shrouded beneath white gauzy fabric. The doctor tells Bumpher her condition is ‘very strange.’ She cannot remember what happened. The townspeople gather, demanding ‘we’ve got a right to know.’ At the college, Miss Lumley calls the news ‘wonderful’; Mrs Appleyard says it ‘makes it worse.’ Analysis : Irma’s return should solve the mystery but instead deepens it. Her lost memory denies rational explanation even to someone who was there. Mrs Appleyard’s reaction reveals her real priority: one survivor with no story is worse for the college than a clear answer of any kind.

Sequence 9 : ‘Tell Us, Irma!’

Summary : Before leaving for Europe, Irma visits the college in a vivid red dress. In the gymnasium, where Sara has been strapped to the wall to cure her stooping, the girls surround Irma demanding answers. The scene becomes hysterical; Miss Lumley cowers behind the piano and Mademoiselle intervenes, slapping Edith. Analysis : Irma’s red links her to the red cloud and suggests she has been transformed. The scene exposes the breakdown of social conventions: fear of the unknown and desperation for answers turn the ‘refined’ young ladies into a mob. Sara’s literal restraint is a stark image of the college’s oppressive control.

Sequence 10 : The College Unravels

Summary : Parents withdraw their daughters. Miss Lumley resigns. Mrs Appleyard drinks alone and tells Sara she must return to the orphanage. Sara is later found dead in the greenhouse by the head gardener. That night Albert dreams of his ‘kid sister’. Mrs Appleyard tells Mademoiselle that Sara’s guardian collected her. Analysis : The greatest damage comes not from the Rock but from within the institution. Mrs Appleyard’s need for control turns into cruelty, and the most vulnerable person pays for it. Her lie about Sara shows her preserving appearances to the end.

Sequence 11 : The Ending

Summary : At dinner, Mrs Appleyard, dressed more softly than ever before, reminisces about Bournemouth, where ‘nothing changed – ever,’ and wonders how Miss McCraw could ‘allow herself to be spirited away.’ A final voice-over reports that the searches continued without result and that Mrs Appleyard died ‘while attempting to climb the rock.’ The film closes on slow-motion images of the picnic, with Miranda turning to wave. Analysis : Mrs Appleyard, the film’s champion of order, is finally drawn to the Rock herself. Her dishevelled hair and black mourning clothes contrast with her immaculate appearance at the start. The closing return to the picnic, filmed to resemble old footage, leaves the audience suspended in the moment before the disappearance, and suggests that nature is timeless while society can be disrupted and destroyed.
