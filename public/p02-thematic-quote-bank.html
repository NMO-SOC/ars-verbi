Nature as a Fundamental Force

  • Miranda (voice-over): ‘What we see and what we seem are but a dream, a dream within a dream.’

  • Mrs Appleyard: ‘The vicinity is renowned for its venomous snakes and poisonous ants of various species.’

  • Mrs Appleyard: ‘The Rock is extremely dangerous, and you are therefore forbidden any tomboy foolishness in the matter of exploration.’

  • Irma, as the Rock comes into view: ‘Waiting a million years, just for us.’

  • Edith: ‘Why can’t we just sit on this log and look at the ugly old rock from here?’

  • Edith: ‘It’s nasty here.’

  • Edith (remembering the Rock): ‘It was a cloud... it was red.’

  • Michael, of the Rock: ‘It comes back at night in dreams.’

  • Sara, in Albert’s dream: ‘I’ve come a long way to see ya, and now I must go.’

  • Sara, after the disappearance: Miranda had many ‘secrets’; ‘she knew she wouldn’t come back.’

The Human Need for Rationality and Control

  • Mr Hussey: ‘No one knows. No one saw her leave. We were asleep.’

  • Sergeant Bumpher (to Edith): ‘Was there a man?’

  • Mrs Bumpher, reporting local gossip: ‘It couldn’t be local... No one around here would do a thing like that.’

  • Mrs Bumpher, to her husband: ‘People just don’t disappear, my dear. Not without good reason.’

  • A townsman outside the police station: ‘We’ve got a bloody right to know.’

  • The doctor, examining Irma: her unmarked feet are ‘very strange’.

  • The doctor, on Irma’s survival: ‘Remarkable.’

  • Mrs Appleyard, of Miss McCraw: how could her ‘masculine intellect’ be ‘spirited away... like a silly schoolgirl’?

  • The head gardener: ‘There’s some questions got answers and some haven’t.’

  • Mrs Appleyard, on Irma being the only girl found: ‘This makes it worse.’

  • Michael, to Albert: ‘I wake up every night in a cold sweat. Just wondering if they’re still alive.’

  • Michael, insisting on searching the Rock himself: the girls ‘may be out there dying of thirst.’

  • Miss Lumley, rebuked by Mrs Appleyard: Irma’s discovery is ‘wonderful news.’

Time

  • Miranda, of her watch: ‘Don’t wear it anymore. Can’t stand the ticking above my heart.’

  • Irma, of Miranda’s watch: she would wear it ‘always... even in the bath.’

  • Mr Hussey: ‘Blowed if me watch hasn’t stopped. Dead on twelve.’

  • Miss McCraw: ‘Stopped at twelve. Never stopped before. Must be something magnetic.’

  • Mr Hussey: ‘Well after two, I’d say. We’d better be careful. I promised Mrs Appleyard I’d have you lot back at the college by eight.’

  • Marion: ‘I should like to make a few measurements of the rock if we have time.’

  • Irma: ‘We’ll be back long before tea.’

  • Miranda: ‘Everything begins and ends at exactly the right time and place.’

  • Edith: ‘When are we going home?’

  • Miranda, to Mademoiselle: ‘We shall only be gone a little while.’

  • Mrs Appleyard, remembering Bournemouth: ‘Nothing changed – ever.’

Class and Civilisation

  • Mrs Appleyard: ‘You may remove your gloves once the drag has passed through Woodend.’

  • Mrs Appleyard: ‘Mr Hussey! I shall expect you back, Miss McCraw and Mademoiselle, at about 8.’

  • Mrs Appleyard: ‘There are several sets of tuition fees impossibly overdue... Sara Waybourne, for instance.’

  • Mrs Appleyard: Appleyard College is not a ‘charitable institution’.

  • Marion, looking down from the Rock: ‘Whatever can those people be doing down there? Like a lot of ants. Surprising the number of human beings are without purpose. Although it is probable they’re performing some function unknown to themselves.’

  • Miss McCraw, as the party settles beneath the Rock: ‘How foolish can human creatures be.’

  • Albert: if ‘the bloody cop and the bloody... tracker and the bloody dog can’t find ‘em, well, no one bloody can.’

  • Albert, to Michael: ‘I say the crude things. You just think ‘em.’

  • Edith, of the Fitzhuberts: they ‘might be the only living creatures in the whole world.’

  • Mrs Appleyard, to Sara: ‘You will be returned to the orphanage.’

  • Sara, repeating Mrs Appleyard’s words to herself: ‘This is not a charitable institution.’

  • Miss Lumley, to Sara: Sara is ‘not to go on the picnic.’

Beauty, Innocence and Appearance

  • Mademoiselle de Poitiers: ‘Now I know... Miranda is a Botticelli angel.’

  • Mademoiselle, embracing Irma on her return: she is ‘prettier than ever’.

  • Mrs Appleyard, to the students: they can ‘thank the Lord for this merciful deliverance of our dear classmate Irma’.

  • Miss Lumley, of strapping Sara to the gymnasium wall: it is ‘for her own good’, to cure her ‘terrible stooping’.
