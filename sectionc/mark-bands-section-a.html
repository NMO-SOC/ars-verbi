Moving Up the Section A Mark Bands

Every band below is taken word for word from the Section A descriptors on the Exam Assessment Criteria page, which the site attributes to VCAA documentation. The descriptors describe what a response does, so use them as the test for your essay. The advice under each band is my own, drawn from the wording of the descriptors, and it is labelled as advice so you can tell it apart from the criteria.

Band 6: what the descriptor asks for

  • “Demonstrates a clear knowledge of the text, including some elements of its ideas, concerns and values and some awareness of structure and language.”

  • “Develops a response to the topic, supported by appropriate evidence from the text.”

  • “Presents a generally organised piece of writing using language that is mostly accurate and appropriate.”

From 6 to 7

The 7 descriptor changes three things. Knowledge moves from “some elements” to “detailed knowledge of the text throughout”. Awareness of structure moves from “some awareness” to “appropriately acknowledging aspects of the structure of the text and its language”. The response moves from “supported by appropriate evidence” to “sustained and well-supported”.
Advice: check that every body paragraph uses the text, not only the first one. Name one structural or language choice in each paragraph, for example a shot, a narrative voice or a repeated image, and say what it does. Keep one argument running through the whole paragraph; do not drop it after the first quotation.

Band 7: what the descriptor asks for

  • “Demonstrates detailed knowledge of the text throughout, including some of its ideas, concerns and values by appropriately acknowledging aspects of the structure of the text and its language.”

  • “Understands the topic, developing a sustained and well-supported response.”

  • “Develops an organised piece of writing using language accurately and appropriately.”

From 7 to 8

The 8 descriptor asks for a “thoughtful reading” that explores ideas “through a discussion of the structure of the text and its language”. The 7 descriptor only asks for “appropriately acknowledging” aspects of structure and language. The 8 descriptor also asks for a “detailed, substantiated and coherent discussion”, where 7 asks for an “organised piece of writing”.
Advice: for each technique you name, explain how it produces the idea in the paragraph. “Jackson uses a calm narrator” is acknowledgement. “The calm narrator makes the stoning look like routine, so the reader sees the violence as ordinary” is discussion. Make sure each paragraph’s last sentence links back to your contention, so the essay reads as one argument and not as three separate points.

Band 8: what the descriptor asks for

  • “Demonstrates a thoughtful reading of the text, exploring its ideas, concerns and values through a discussion of the structure of the text and its language.”

  • “Demonstrates an understanding of the implications of the topic, exploring it from the basis of the text.”

  • “Develops a detailed, substantiated and coherent discussion using language fluently and confidently.”

From 8 to 9 or 10

The 9–10 descriptor adds “close and perceptive” reading, which considers “complexities of its ideas, concerns and values”. It also asks for an “appropriate strategy” for dealing with the topic and for exploring its “complexity”. The top band also asks for “precise and expressive language”, where 8 asks for language used “fluently and confidently”.
Advice: qualify your contention. Show the strongest evidence against your view, then explain why your view still holds, or where it needs limiting. Test your word choice: replace a general verb such as “shows” with the specific action the text performs, such as “silences”, “exposes” or “normalises”.

Band 9–10: what the descriptor asks for

  • “Demonstrates a close and perceptive reading of the text, considering complexities of its ideas, concerns and values explored through the structure of the text and its language.”

  • “Demonstrates a clear understanding of the implications of the topic, using an appropriate strategy for dealing with it, and exploring its complexity from the basis of the text.”

  • “Develops a cogent, controlled and well-substantiated discussion using precise and expressive language.”

Using the samples to practise

The low, medium and high Section A samples on the Written Exemplars pages are a good place to practise. Read each one against the table above, decide which band the descriptors point to, and write down the single change that would move it up one band. Then compare your answer with the next sample. The exemplars are the best check of your own judgement.
