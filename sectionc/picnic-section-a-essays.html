Section A Sample Essays: Picnic at Hanging Rock (Low, Medium and High)

Topic: Weir suggests that the order imposed by Appleyard College cannot contain the natural world. To what extent do you agree?
Sample Essay I (Low)

Introduction

In Picnic at Hanging Rock, Peter Weir shows a school trying to control nature and failing. I agree with the statement because the college’s rules, its clocks and its sounds all break down when the girls go to the Rock. Weir uses setting, time and sound to show this.
Body Paragraph 1

Weir shows that the college’s rules mean little against the power of nature. Mrs Appleyard has many rules for the picnic. She tells the girls to return ‘at about 8’ and warns them about snakes. This shows the college wants to control the girls. However, when they get to the Rock the rules do not work anymore because the Rock is very big. Weir shows the Rock in big shots so it looks powerful.
Body Paragraph 2 The rules meant to protect the girls are the first thing the Rock overrides.

Time is another way the college tries to control things. Mr Hussey’s and Miss McCraw’s watches stop at twelve, and Miss McCraw says it ‘must be something magnetic’. This shows the Rock is stronger than the clocks. Irma says they will be back ‘long before tea’, but they never come back, so the college’s plans fail.
Body Paragraph 3 So the order the college relies on fails at the very point it is meant to hold.

Weir’s soundtrack makes nature seem both beautiful and dangerous. The music is calm at first, then there is a scary noise. This shows nature is beautiful but also dangerous. When Edith runs, she screams, which makes the audience feel scared.
Conclusion The threat of the Rock is therefore something the audience hears as well as sees.

Weir shows the college trying to control nature and failing. The rules, the time and the sound all show this. Therefore I agree with the statement.
Sample Essay II (Medium)

Introduction

In Picnic at Hanging Rock, Peter Weir presents Appleyard College as a place built on order: its timetables, its rules about gloves and punctuality, its certainty about when the girls will return. I agree that the film shows this order failing against nature, though the failure is not a simple victory for the Rock. Weir uses time, framing and sound to show an ordered world losing its hold on the people inside it.
Body Paragraph 1

The college lives by the clock, and the picnic is planned around it. Mrs Appleyard tells the girls to return ‘at about 8’, and Irma is confident they will be back ‘long before tea’. At the Rock, though, the clocks stop. Mr Hussey’s and Miss McCraw’s watches stop at twelve, and Miss McCraw can only suggest that it ‘must be something magnetic’. The mechanical measures of the college become meaningless in a place that seems to run on a different time.
Body Paragraph 2 The college’s order therefore depends on a time that the Rock does not share.

Weir also reduces the girls’ authority through framing. As they climb, low-angle shots make the pinnacles tower over them, and narrow passages enclose them. The girls are shown gazing upwards, enthralled, yet the same shots make them look small. The college’s rules about gloves and snakes are trivial beside a landscape that seems to be swallowing them.
Body Paragraph 3 Framing, then, turns the college’s authority into something the landscape can overwhelm.

The soundtrack completes the picture. Gheorghe Zamfir’s pan flute makes the bush seem romantic, but beneath it Weir layers a low rumble that seems to come from the Rock. When Edith flees, her screams merge with a shrieking noise. Beauty and threat are never separated, so the audience cannot fully trust the bush.
Conclusion The soundtrack therefore carries the film’s argument that order cannot contain nature.

Weir presents Appleyard College as an order that cannot survive contact with the Rock. Its clocks stop, its rules shrink and its voices are drowned out by the sound of the bush. The film does not give nature a simple victory, however. The girls are not conquered so much as taken, and that is what makes the order’s failure so unsettling.
Sample Essay III (High)

Introduction

The opening of Picnic at Hanging Rock sets up its argument before the college appears at all. Weir begins with the Rock emerging from mist and Miranda’s voice-over: ‘What we see and what we seem are but a dream, a dream within a dream.’ I agree with the statement, but the film’s argument is harder than a story of defeat. The order of Appleyard College is not overpowered by the Rock. It is revealed as a fiction the moment it meets the bush. Weir builds this through the opening, the stopped clocks and the soundtrack.
Body Paragraph 1

The opening makes that fiction visible. Before the title card gives its factual details, the Rock is shown first, and Miranda’s voice-over frames everything that follows as a dream. Weir inverts the usual hierarchy: nature is presented as the older, more real authority, and the college is an afterthought. When the girls later climb the Rock, they walk into the same dream and accept it without question.
Body Paragraph 2 The opening therefore settles the film’s answer before the disappearance begins.

Time shows most clearly how the college’s order gives way to the Rock. The clocks provide the clearest evidence. Appleyard College runs on instructions to return ‘at about 8’, and Irma is sure they will be back ‘long before tea’. At the Rock, Mr Hussey’s and Miss McCraw’s watches stop at twelve, and Miss McCraw can only suggest that it ‘must be something magnetic’. Miranda goes further, rejecting her own watch because its ticking is unbearable, and at the summit she declares that ‘everything begins and ends at exactly the right time and place’. Weir shows the college’s measured time as a convention the Rock simply does not recognise.
Body Paragraph 3 The college’s clocks are the first thing the Rock makes meaningless.

Sound finishes what time begins. Gheorghe Zamfir’s pan flute drifts over the crossing of the stream, making the bush seem freely romantic, but Weir layers a low rumble beneath it and lets Edith’s screams merge with a shrieking discord. The audience is never allowed to rest in the beauty. Even after Irma is found, the rush of wind through the rocks keeps the question open. The college wants an explanation, and the film offers only sensation.
Conclusion That refusal to explain is what makes the college’s failure complete.

Weir’s film argues that order is a story people tell themselves, and the Rock refuses to co-operate with it. The opening dream, the stopped watches and the shrieking soundtrack each undermine the college’s authority in a different register. Because the film never explains the disappearance, the failure of Appleyard College is not a single moment. It is the condition the whole film describes.
