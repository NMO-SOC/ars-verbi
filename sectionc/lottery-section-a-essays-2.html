Section A Sample Essays: The Lottery (Low, Medium and High)

Topic: Jackson suggests that tradition is more powerful than reason. Do you agree?
Sample Essay I (Low)

Introduction

In ‘The Lottery’, Shirley Jackson shows a village that keeps a ritual even though nobody can explain why. I agree that tradition is more powerful than reason in the story. Jackson uses the villagers’ sayings, the black box and the response to Tessie Hutchinson to show this.

Body Paragraph 1

Old Man Warner says ‘There’s always been a lottery’. This shows that tradition is important to the villagers. He also calls the people who want to stop ‘Pack of crazy fools’. This shows that tradition wins over reason. Tradition is what keeps the lottery going. So the village keeps the lottery because it has always done so, not because it makes sense.

Body Paragraph 2

The saying ‘Lottery in June, corn be heavy soon’ is repeated as if it were a reason. Nobody questions it. This shows the saying is more powerful than thinking. So reason never gets a hearing in the village.

Body Paragraph 3

At the end, the villagers pick up stones, and ‘Come on, come on, everyone’ is said. This shows tradition is stronger than the people. So the ending proves the same point.

Conclusion

Jackson shows tradition is more powerful than reason. So I agree with the statement.

Sample Essay II (Medium)

Introduction

In ‘The Lottery’, Shirley Jackson presents a village that continues a ritual no one can justify. I agree that tradition is more powerful than reason, though the story suggests that tradition survives partly through fear. Jackson builds this through the saying the villagers repeat, the black box nobody will replace and the way the crowd answers Tessie Hutchinson’s appeal to fairness.

Body Paragraph 1

Jackson shows that the villagers keep the lottery because of what they have always done, not because of any reason they can give. Old Man Warner insists ‘There’s always been a lottery’, and the proverb ‘Lottery in June, corn be heavy soon’ is repeated as though it explains everything. The proverb ties the ritual to the harvest, a practical concern, but nobody checks whether the connection is real; the saying is accepted because it is old. Tradition therefore has force even where the reason behind it has disappeared.

Body Paragraph 2

The black box shows how far the village has let tradition outlast its meaning. The narration notes that no one liked to upset ‘even as much tradition as was represented by the black box’, and Mr Summers’s repeated talk of making a new box never leads to one. The village would rather keep an old box than change a ritual whose reason has been lost. The object carries the practice when the reasons have gone.

Body Paragraph 3

Reason is answered with social pressure, not argument. When Tessie protests ‘It isn’t fair, it isn’t right’, Mrs Delacroix replies ‘Be a good sport, Tessie’, and Mrs Graves adds that ‘All of us took the same chance.’ Neither reply addresses whether the drawing was fair; both appeal to belonging. So when reason is raised, tradition answers with the pressure of the group, and the ritual continues.

Conclusion

Jackson shows that the lottery survives because tradition has outlived its reasons and reason has no way to win against the group. I agree, though fear of exclusion is part of why tradition wins.

Sample Essay III (High)

Introduction

Shirley Jackson opens ‘The Lottery’ with a morning that is ‘clear and sunny’, and with a village that gathers without asking why. I agree that tradition is more powerful than reason, but the story shows that this power is not simply habit. Jackson builds it through the proverbs the villagers repeat, the black box they will not replace and the social pressure that answers Tessie Hutchinson’s appeal to fairness.

Body Paragraph 1

The proverbs carry tradition as if it were knowledge. The saying ‘Lottery in June, corn be heavy soon’ links the ritual to survival, and Old Man Warner warns that without it ‘we’d all be eating stewed chickweed and acorns’. The claim is a warning rather than an argument. It sets the ritual against hunger, which is why questioning the lottery sounds reckless. Reason has no part in a proverb that presents the ritual as the thing that keeps people fed, and so tradition gains its power by seeming practical.

Body Paragraph 2

The black box shows that tradition can outlast its own materials. The narration says the villagers ‘had forgotten the ritual and lost the original black box, they still remembered to use stones’. The ritual has become detached from its objects, yet its violence remains. Tradition persists in what it demands even when its form has gone, and the stones are the one part of the ritual the village has never let go of.

Body Paragraph 3

Reason has no hearing once the crowd has decided. Tessie’s argument is about fairness, ‘It isn’t fair, it isn’t right’, and she is answered with ‘Be a good sport, Tessie’ and ‘All of us took the same chance.’ The replies concern belonging, not fairness. ‘Come on, come on, everyone’ turns the crowd towards her, and ‘A stone hit her on the side of the head’ shows where the argument ends. So tradition wins not by being right but by being shared.

Conclusion

Jackson shows that tradition is more powerful than reason because it does not need to be justified: it survives in proverbs, in objects and in the pressure to belong. I agree, though the story also shows what that power costs.
